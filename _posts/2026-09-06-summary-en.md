### [Asahi Linux Reaches Official Apple M3 Support](https://asahilinux.org/2026/09/m2-episode-1/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Phoronix report attached to this item says Asahi Linux has reached official support for Apple M3 chips, expanding the Linux port’s coverage to that Apple Silicon generation. The project continues to rely on reverse engineering Apple’s custom silicon, and commenters view the effort as significant. Practical limitations remain, however: users mention missing sleep and HDMI support on Macs, and one llama.cpp user reports worse performance than with Apple’s Metal backend. hackernews · mdp2021 · Sep 6, 14:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49586698)

**「Background」** Asahi Linux is a community project that ports Linux to Apple Silicon Macs by reverse-engineering Apple&\#x27;s proprietary hardware and firmware. It previously focused on the M1 and M2 series machines. The recent announcement adds official support for M3, M3 Pro, and M3 Max chips, though upstreaming this support into mainline Linux is still ongoing.

**「Impact」** The most direct consequence is for owners of Apple M3 Macs who want Linux: an official Asahi target is now available, giving them a supported path rather than a purely experimental one. Still, the reported gaps in sleep, HDMI, and GPU-related performance mean many users may continue to rely on macOS for some everyday tasks.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters are enthusiastic about the reverse-engineering achievement, comparing it to fixing a spaceship mid-launch, while practical blockers such as absent sleep and HDMI support and poor llama.cpp performance versus Metal remain concerns. Others ask about dual-booting macOS and Asahi on Apple silicon, suggesting that workflow is attractive once functionality catches up.
