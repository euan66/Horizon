### [California Governor Signs Order Seeking Mandatory Reporting of AI Loss-of-Control Incidents](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/usstock/c/2026-09-19/doc-inisisqc3124180.shtml) ⭐️ 8.0/10

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order on September 19 aimed at strengthening AI safety, including a proposed requirement that companies report &quot;loss-of-control incidents&quot; involving AI agents and a possible mandate that advanced models be equipped with emergency shutdown mechanisms. The order convenes a panel of experts to issue guidance within two months on how to improve AI safety law, and it proposes periodic audits of AI laboratories. Newsom said federal regulation has been insufficient, making state-level action necessary. The measures are framed as proposals, guidance, and possible requirements rather than binding rules, and the account comes from a brief secondary report by Sina Finance, so the order&\#x27;s precise scope and enforceability remain unclear.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 19, 05:44

**「Background」** California is home to several of the largest frontier AI developers, which makes state-level rules there potentially far-reaching for the industry. The order follows a period in which comprehensive federal AI safety legislation has not been enacted; the source attributes the action to Newsom&\#x27;s stated view that federal oversight is insufficient. The measures described — incident reporting, emergency shutdown capability, and lab audits — are framed as guidance and proposals rather than finalized requirements, so their binding scope remains to be determined over the two-month expert review period.

**「Impact」** Frontier AI developers operating in California would face onsite independent verification organizations conducting regular audits and evaluations, expanded reporting of incidents in which they lose control of AI systems, and a state auditor registry and oversight program accelerated from January 2029 to December 2027. Because these measures are framed as directions for proposed state law rather than enacted requirements, their binding force remains uncertain.

### [Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google sued over calls to slow AI development](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/18/anthropic-openai-spacexai-google-sued-over-calls-to-pace-ai-development-01085023) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google face a federal antitrust class-action lawsuit in California filed by consumers who subscribe to their AI services, alleging the companies&\#x27; executives publicly backed a coordinated slowdown in frontier AI development, which the complaint says could be an illegal agreement to restrain competition under Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The plaintiffs are seeking class certification and an injunction, and the four companies have not responded to requests for comment.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 19, 02:08

**「Background」** The complaint follows a public post this month by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei urging the industry to jointly slow frontier AI development, which the suit says Elon Musk, Sam Altman and Demis Hassabis then publicly endorsed. It invokes Section 1 of the Sherman Act, which bars competing companies from agreeing among themselves to restrain competition.

### [Beijing regulator opens investigation into Meituan, Fliggy, Tongcheng and Tujia over hotel algorithm marketing](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/FsHQ-AG2zSNSWfA2sJAXfQ) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On September 19, Beijing&\#x27;s Municipal Market Supervision Administration opened an investigation into Meituan, Fliggy, Tongcheng and Tujia over alleged violations in their hotel accommodation business, including paid ranking of listing traffic and requirements that merchants sell at a platform-wide lowest price, which the investigation treats as stripping merchants of pricing autonomy. All four companies said they will cooperate with regulators; no penalties or financial impact have been disclosed.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 19, 07:47

**「Background」** The Beijing Municipal Market Supervision Administration had already inspected the four companies in April, and the formal case follows a State Administration for Market Regulation deployment and preliminary checks under China’s anti-unfair competition and e-commerce laws, according to CCTV News reports cited by Sina and 17173.

**「Who is directly affected」** The businesses most directly affected are hotels and other accommodation merchants listing on these four platforms, because the practices under investigation — paying to bid for traffic display and being required to sell at the lowest price across all sites — shape their pricing freedom and how visible they are to travelers.
